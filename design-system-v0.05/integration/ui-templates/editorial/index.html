Inside the printed page, a tightly packed rectangle holds black lines and ochre marks. Circles, triangles, tiny faces, and bands of repeated strokes sit close together; paper margins and Arabic lettering remain visible around the composition.

A field within a page

The image does not begin at the edge of its artwork. A handwritten line runs above it, while larger outlined lettering appears below. A narrow strip of color and texture crosses the very top. Those margins give the geometric field a clear boundary without separating it from the page that carries it.

Within the rectangle, a dark branching shape fills much of the left side. Across the upper middle and right, smaller forms overlap a mesh of short marks. Near the center, a square-faced figure stands among roundels and pointed shapes. The picture rewards a slow scan because no single element occupies the whole field.

Circles gather. Triangles answer. A zigzag carries the eye across.

A changing rhythm

Along the lower half, two large ringed circles sit above vertical stripes. A long curved form passes nearby, enclosed by dots and rows of small triangles. At the right edge, straight bars and a fine grid make a sharper counterpoint to the loops and arcs.

The same shapes change character as their neighbors change: open rings become markers, repeated triangles become a border, and dense hatchwork turns into a dark mass. Ochre patches interrupt the mostly black drawing and pull attention across the page.

Looking from edge to edge

Try following one path at a time. Trace the zigzag band, then return to the small circular marks around it. Look at the right-hand grid, then compare it with the loose field of lines on the left. The composition offers these routes without requiring one fixed reading order.
