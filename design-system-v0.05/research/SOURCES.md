The Islamic p. 183 / PDF p. 205 discussion supplies repeat logic rather than another motif: periodic plane patterns can be analyzed with translation, rotation, reflection, and glide reflection across 17 wallpaper groups. Bonner explicitly cautions that these are geometric descriptions, not proof that historical artisans used crystallographic categories as their generative method. Use the framework to test seam continuity and symmetry; do not imply a historical construction method from a matching symmetry label alone.

## Integration guardrails

- Keep motif source, named figure/page, redraw status, and rights/provenance adjacent in the asset record. The Ancient booklet pages have no verified artifact-level source details in this review.
- Separate the three visual vocabularies at first: square-grid/ordered borders, floral/pendant friezes, and star/tessellation geometry. Combine them only after a deliberate pattern study; avoid a generic “Egyptian-Islamic” mash-up.
- For repeats, test all four tile edges, identify the repeat cell and symmetry operations, and retain the original module proportions. Do not force fivefold or eightfold forms into a fourfold square just for layout convenience.
- Start with monochrome source studies. Apply the GMD Nile/pink palette as a separate contemporary design layer; those colors do not establish historical color use.
- Mark newly drawn outcomes “modern derivative / source study.” Verify rights for reproduction before publication; this note is not a license determination.
