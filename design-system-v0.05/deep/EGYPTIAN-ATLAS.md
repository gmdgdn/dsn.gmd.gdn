# Egyptian booklet visual atlas for GMD v0.05

This is a visual-design reading of the complete 29-page scanned booklet [Ancient Egyptian Designs: Inspiration for textiles, ceramics and printing etc.](<C:/Users/khorm/Downloads/79176a587b864217-high.pdf>). The source is image-only. The cover gives a title but no verified author, date, plate captions, or object-level provenance. PDF page numbers below refer to the file's page order; no printed folios were verified. Shape names here describe what the drawings look like and how they are built. They do not identify artifacts, periods, sites, or historic uses.

The existing GMD v0.05 visual system uses ink `#140206`, paper `#F7F4EF`, Nile blue `#1034A6`, pink `#FF6183`, editorial serif with sans, raster labels, compartment navigation, hard `5px 3px 0` shadows, and stripe windows. The booklet's monochrome plates can supply shape studies. Apply the GMD palette as a present-day design layer; it is not evidence of historical color.

## A usable shape grammar

1. **Choose a base cell.** The booklet offers circle, half-circle, square, pointed pendant, and narrow vertical strip. Let the selected cell determine the crop and repeat direction.
2. **Choose one motion.** Stack, radiate, hang, turn at right angles, interlace, or spiral. Avoid mixing multiple motion rules in a single small region.
3. **Set the beat.** Alternate filled and open cells, or vary center marks while keeping the outer unit fixed. Use a deliberate blank cell or blank interval to make text space.
4. **Separate outline from fill.** Preserve a clear ink contour, paper void, and one contemporary color accent. Dense fills belong in framed windows; body text needs an unpatterned field.
5. **Frame before repeating.** Establish an outer rectangle, circle, or viewport crop. The booklet frequently demonstrates one isolated unit beside a repeat; use that transition to frame a feature, rather than tiling every available surface.
6. **Align the repeat to the page grid.** Square and circular patterns can sit inside cards or compartment navigation; vertical strips can align with section rows; long friezes can key to title or footer baselines. Cut repeats on unit seams.

## Suggested GMD web placements

- **Landing hero:** Use the p. 8 radial fan as an oversized top canopy with its center just offscreen. Place the serif heading in a clear paper opening, with one Nile or pink disk and the existing hard shadow on a framed callout.
- **Compartment navigation:** Use p. 10/11 square modules for small section markers. Keep the label in a plain square cell and vary only neighboring centers, so the navigation remains quick to scan.
- **Editorial feature:** Frame p. 2's triptych or one p. 5 medallion as an image panel. Use serif for the title and sans for metadata; let the image's internal compartments echo the page's content groups.
- **Section divider:** Hang a p. 12/16 pendant register below a title, or run a p. 17-style narrow strip beside the section. Retain visible negative space between the pattern and text.
- **Research/story panel:** Show the p. 26-to-27 transition as a contemporary “grid to repeat” study. Put captions and any source note on a separate paper panel.
- **Cards or statistics:** Put one p. 23/25 rosette around a numeral or image crop, leaving the center calm. Keep pattern density out of paragraph backgrounds.

Use the raster samples as visual references, not as vetted historical objects or a reproduction license. The booklet's final page (p. 29) says “for educational purposes only”; this atlas does not establish copyright status or permission to reproduce. Cite the source page with any derivative study and label newly drawn work as a modern design study.

## Full-booklet page map

- **p. 1:** Cover/title page; no author or date verified.

- **pp. 9–11:** Square-grid meanders, nested squares, and repeated square modules with varying centers.
- **pp. 12–16:** Horizontal registers of oval, pointed, scalloped, rosette, and pendant forms.
- **pp. 17–19:** Narrow vertical pattern strips built from spirals, rosettes, fans, and stacked modules.
- **pp. 20–21:** Horizontal repeats from interlocking circular arcs, diamond gaps, and centers.
- **p. 22:** Dense continuous rounded ribbon / interlacing path repeat.
- **pp. 23–25:** Circle and rosette fields with concentric rings, dots, fans, and pointed separators.
- **pp. 26–27:** Square construction grids resolving into spiral-and-diamond repeats.
- **p. 28:** Blank.
- **p. 29:** Educational-purpose note.
